Nayarit, where the land embraces the sea

Nayarit is a small but vibrant state on Mexico’s Pacific coast. Its capital is Tepic, and its identity is shaped by beaches, mountains, Indigenous culture, and unforgettable flavors.

Nayarit Overview

Location and identity

Nayarit is a state located in western Mexico on the Pacific coast. Its capital is Tepic, and it is known for the Riviera Nayarit, including Sayulita, Punta Mita, Nuevo Vallarta, and San Pancho, with a strong connection to surfing, sport fishing, and ecotourism.

Culture

The Huichol people preserve unique crafts and rituals, keeping Nayarit’s cultural traditions alive and vibrant.

Climate, flora, and fauna

The climate ranges from tropical on the coast to milder in the mountains. The state’s landscapes include tropical forests, mangroves, palm trees, cacti, and bougainvillea, while wildlife features crocodiles, iguanas, deer, jaguars in protected areas, sea turtles, tropical birds, and many fish species.

Food

Nayarit’s food is famous for seafood like Nayarit-style ceviche, coconut shrimp, and grilled fish, along with shrimp tacos, tejuino, and corn tamales. Sweet favorites include cocadas, milk candies, and fruit empanadas.

Places and tourism

Visitors explore the Marietas Islands and the Hidden Beach, the Sierra de Nayarit’s mountains and waterfalls, and Tepic’s history, museums, and cuisine. People come for beaches, water sports, whale watching, hiking, and Indigenous culture—reasons why Nayarit is called the “Riviera Nayarit” and the “Land of Traditions.”
